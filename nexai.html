Nexai

Vibe-Coding · AI experiment · Interaction design

Nexai was developed as part of MIX301 during my master’s degree in Media and Interaction Design at the University of Bergen. The course focused on exploring how AI can be integrated into creative and professional workflows, and how working with AI can influence both the design process and the resulting user experience.

Throughout the project, we experimented with AI as a design and development tool, exploring how it could support ideation, prototyping and the creation of a functional product. This also allowed us to reflect on how AI-assisted workflows affect design decisions, collaboration and the way users interact with the final product.

Project

Nexai — Vibe-Coding

Nexai is an AI-assisted photography application designed to support photographers and designers during the process of image composition. The application uses AI to provide real-time visual feedback and suggestions, helping users evaluate elements such as composition, framing, and aesthetic balance. Rather than replacing the designer's creative decisions, Nexai positions AI as a mediating layer between the user, the camera, and the emerging image. Its goal is to explore how AI can influence creative decision-making while keeping the designer actively involved in interpreting, accepting, or rejecting algorithmic suggestions.

Design question

What did you want to learn or test?

How can AI mediate the designer's creative process in photography, and how does this mediation influence composition, aesthetic judgment, and the designer's role in making visual decisions?

Core interaction

Key Features & Interactions

Nexai is designed as an AI-assisted photography tool that supports users throughout the creative process. Rather than making decisions for the photographer, the system provides contextual feedback that helps users experiment, reflect and improve their photography.

Live AI feedback

Nexai uses AI as a real-time creative assistant rather than an automatic decision-maker. While composing a shot, the system analyses the scene and provides contextual suggestions about framing, subject placement, focus, and exposure. The photographer remains in control, choosing whether to follow, ignore, or interpret the feedback. After the photo is taken, Nexai provides a more detailed analysis, highlighting what worked, what could be improved, and why. This creates a feedback loop where each photograph becomes an opportunity to reflect, experiment, and develop the user's photographic judgement.

Observe → Suggest → Decide → Capture → Reflect → Improve

After-photo review

After capturing an image, Nexai provides a structured AI analysis of the photograph. The review combines an overall quality score with positive observations and actionable suggestions, helping the photographer understand both what worked and what could be improved. Rather than simply judging the image, the mechanic is designed to encourage reflection and learning. The user can compare the AI's assessment with their own aesthetic judgement and use the feedback to inform their next shot.

Capture → Analyse → Understand → Reflect → Apply

Gamification

Nexai uses gamification to turn photographic improvement into an ongoing journey. Users earn points, unlock achievements, complete challenges, and track their progress as they experiment with different photographic techniques. The mechanic is designed to motivate exploration rather than competition. By making improvement visible and rewarding continued experimentation, NexAI encourages users to keep practicing and develop their photographic confidence over time.

Experiment → Complete → Earn → Progress → Improve
Prototyping

Ideation

We explored different ideas based on our interests and experimented with how these concepts could translate into an interactive experience. Through multiple rounds of prototyping, we developed and refined the core interactions, visual direction and functionality of the game.

Testing & Feedback

We conducted scenario-based testing with an amateur photographer, using tasks such as indoor portrait photography, close-up photography and capturing a landscape through a window. The testing helped us evaluate how users responded to both the real-time AI guidance and the after-photo review.

The results showed that short, concrete feedback such as “Nice framing” worked well, while vague prompts like “Keep adjusting” could create frustration when displayed for too long. Visual elements such as AI Vision and loading indicators also added distractions during live feedback. In contrast, the after-photo review was perceived as the most useful feature, helping users understand what worked, what could be improved, and apply suggestions to their next photos.

The testing also showed that gamification and scoring could be motivating, but needed to remain subtle so they did not distract from the creative process. Overall, the findings suggested that users benefit most from short, actionable feedback, while real-time AI should support rather than replace their own judgement.

Based on the feedback, we implemented a “Minimal feedback information” setting that reduces the amount of AI guidance displayed during photography. This helped minimise distractions and was quickly implemented using AI-assisted development.

Design takeaways

Key lessons and Future work

Developing Nexai showed both the potential and limitations of AI-assisted photography. Using Apple Core ML together with tools such as FigmaMake and Cursor allowed us to rapidly prototype, test and iterate on ideas, but also revealed that real-time AI feedback can still be vague or inconsistent.

User testing showed that live guidance needs to become more precise and actionable to be truly useful during photography. Future iterations could explore more capable AI systems, such as Gemini, to provide richer and more context-aware feedback.

We also explored the possibility of integrating Nexai directly with DSLR and mirrorless cameras. While this was beyond the scope of our prototype, camera-level integration could be an interesting direction for future development..

Moving forward: Key priorities would be:

  • Refine: Make AI feedback more precise and actionable.
  • Explore: Investigate more capable AI systems and camera integration.
  • Validate: Test with a wider range of photographers and real-world scenarios.

The mechanic is simple: rushing makes the world harder. Slowing down gives it space to recover.
